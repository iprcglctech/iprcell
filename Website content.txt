About Us


1. About GLC (College’s image)


Established in 1855, Government Law College (GLC), Mumbai is a landmark institution in legal education and is recognised as Asia’s oldest law college. With a legacy built on academic excellence, practical legal training, and a commitment to shaping future legal professionals, GLC has played a defining role in India’s legal landscape.
Its distinguished alumni include Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, Justice M.C. Chagla, Nani Palkhivala, Ram Jethmalani, and Fali S. Nariman, reflecting the institution’s enduring influence across the Bar, Judiciary, academia, and public life.
Today, GLC continues to combine its rich heritage with a forward-looking approach, equipping students with the knowledge, skills, and practical exposure needed to navigate an evolving legal world.

2. About IPRC 
The college has decided to inaugurate an Intellectual Property Rights Cell (IPRC) in the academic year of 2023-2024. This initiative embarks on a mission to illuminate students and innovators with a thorough understanding about the intricate realm of intellectual property rights and create a platform for engagement, while concurrently nurturing a self-sustainable environment that thrives on innovation and creativity within the college ecosystem. As nations worldwide intensify their emphasis on innovation, start-ups, research, and collaborative endeavours, the imperative to comprehensively understand intellectual property rights and safeguard inventive creations has surged among students. Recognising this surge in significance, the institution aims to ingrain foundational awareness of IPR at the very outset of students’ college journeys. By championing a holistic approach, the committee seeks to provide students with a well-rounded education encompassing both theoretical understanding and practical application in the realm of intellectual property. The committee uses various competitions, research papers, certificate courses, lectures by experts, and pro bono work as a means to achieve this goal.
FOUNDER’S MESSAGE (Gaurav’s image)
I watched it happen too often, batchmates and seniors writing off IP as a career before really giving it a chance, assuming it did not pay or did not matter. In law school, IP rarely went beyond the syllabus. Businesses carried the same blind spot, treating IP as something to sort out later, after there was actually something worth protecting. By then, someone else had already filed the mark, already owned the idea. That is why I started the IPR Cell in 2023, to make at least a few students look twice. The ones who take IP seriously now will be the ones protecting it later, in boardrooms, in courtrooms, and in businesses of their own.

Trademark Opposition Competition


India's first-of-its-kind Trade Mark Opposition Competition (TOC) is organized by the Intellectual Property Rights Cell of Government Law College (GLC), Mumbai. The competition is designed as a simulation of trademark opposition proceedings under the Trade Marks Act, 1999 and the Trade Marks Rules of 2017 to enhance participants' research, drafting, and oral advocacy skills in Intellectual Property Law. The competition's focus on both the substantive provisions of the Trade Marks Act, 1999  and the procedural rules governing opposition proceedings provides comprehensive coverage of India's trademark ecosystem. This dual approach ensures that participants develop not only knowledge of legal principles but also the practical skills needed to navigate the trademark registration and enforcement processes effectively. 


The competition is hosted in commemoration of Prof. Tehemtan N. Daruwalla, whose legacy continues to inspire excellence in the field of law. Prof. Daruwalla completed his LL.B. from Government Law College, Mumbai, before going on to become a Solicitor in 1960 and choosing to practice the then lesser-known field of Trade Mark and Patent Law a path less trodden, but one on which he left a very big mark. He went on to teach the LL.M. course in Intellectual Property at Mumbai University from 1976, and taught at GLC Mumbai from 1979 to 1992, shaping generations of IP lawyers who now carry forward his work. The Trademark Opposition Competition stands as GLC's tribute to his enduring legacy in Indian intellectual property law. 


INTER TOC (add images)


Inter TOC Competition is open for students pursuing LLB or BA LLB (equivalent integrated degrees) across India. The competition consists of 2 phases. It starts with a written submissions round in line with Rules 42 to 47 under the Trade Marks Rules, 2017, read with Section 21 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999, to challenge drafting skills, ability to analyse the matter and presenting in a comprehensible manner, which further proceeds to an oral round in line with Rule 50 under the Trade Marks Rule, 2017, read with Section 21 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999, which is conducted offline at the GLC, Mumbai campus.[a] The oral round will challenge your advocacy, your knowledge of the matter and how you argue using that. INTRA-TOC (add images)


The Intra-TOC is specifically for students of GLC Mumbai. It consists only of oral rounds, conducted as per Rule 50 of the Trade Mark Rules 2017 read with Section 21 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999, thus excluding the written rounds. This format focuses exclusively on oral advocacy before the Registrar, reflecting actual trademark opposition hearings. The Intra-TOC is designed to hone students’ oral argument skills within the institution. This streamlined procedural approach helps GLC students focus on effective presentation and argumentation in trademark opposition cases, reinforcing their understanding of the procedural aspects under the Trade Marks Act and providing practical courtroom experience.

IPR Journal 
The IPR Journal, published by the IPR Cell of Government Law College, Mumbai, is a student-led academic initiative in Intellectual Property Law and allied areas like competition law, AI, and technology law. Published annually, it invites law students, researchers, and academicians to submit original, unpublished work on contemporary debates, landmark judgments, and emerging IP challenges.
All submissions undergo a double-blind peer review to ensure academic rigour and quality. The journal is known for its focus on originality and sharp legal analysis, giving young scholars a platform to sharpen their research and writing skills. In partnership with CaseMine and Manupatra, the IPR Journal continues to nurture research-driven legal minds shaping the future of IP law and policy.




CFP 2026-27 (TBD)
The journal seeks original and unpublished works focused on Intellectual Property Law and allied areas such as competition law, artificial intelligence, and technology law. Types of submissions accepted include:
* Articles (5,000–6,000 words): Providing a comprehensive analysis with suggestions and an abstract of 200–250 words.
* Essays (3,000–5,000 words): Offering fresh perspectives on common issues with an abstract of 150–200 words.
* Case Comments (2,500–3,000 words): Critiquing key judgments with an abstract of 150–200 words.
Submissions may have up to two authors and must strictly adhere to Bluebook (21st edition) citation standards, using footnotes for references while avoiding speaking footnotes. All manuscripts must be free of author-identifying information to ensure anonymity in double-blind review. Formatting requirements include:
* Times New Roman font
* Font size 12 for content, 10 for footnotes
* 1.5 line spacing (content), 1.0 (footnotes)
* Justified and capitalized headings
* Header stating the journal’s name and footer with page numbers


A cover letter separately stating title, author names, position, institutional affiliation, and contact details is mandatory. Manuscripts must be submitted in Word format after registering via the provided Google Form. No registration or publication fees apply, and eligibility is open to individuals enrolled in any professional course. Selection criteria include originality, research methodology, plagiarism threshold (below 10%), relevance, and research quality.

WHY IPRC?
Ever wondered who decides that a brand, a song, or an invention actually belongs to someone? That's what we do here.
The IPR Cell at GLC Mumbai is for anyone curious about patents, copyrights, trademarks, and the newer stuff — AI, data, digital rights  that most classrooms haven't caught up to yet.
You don't need to know it all. Just show up wanting to learn.
What you get:
* Real depth in a field that's only getting bigger.
* Actual hands-on work, not just theory.
* A shot at our flagship event.
* Create a Professional Network
* Build a profile that stands out
The IPR Cell has a structured membership process that aims to develop competence and leadership in intellectual property law: -
   * Junior Members: This entry-level post is open to students in their V-I and III-I years at Government Law College in Mumbai and allows for active participation in the Cell's projects. Junior Members will receive experience in various facets of intellectual property law through workshops, competitions, and collaborative projects.
   * Junior Core Members (JC): At the conclusion of the academic year, selected Junior Members will be elevated to Junior Core Members. In this capacity, members will be given more tasks, such as event management, research, and content development, which will help the IPR Cell expand its reach and influence.
   * Senior Core Members (SC): Outstanding Junior Core Members will be promoted to Senior Core Members. These members will be empowered to lead within the Cell, taking responsibility for many of the strategic initiatives, supervising Junior Members, and defining the vision and strategy of the Cell
ELIGIBILITY GUIDELINES
Applicants wishing to join the IPR Cell as Junior Members must possess the following qualifications:
* Educational Qualification: Students of V-I or III-I at Government Law College, Mumbai are eligible to apply.
* Interest in the Field: The applicant should demonstrate a good understanding and interest in intellectual property law, specifically how it operates in the global market.
* Personal Attributes: The IPR Cell seeks all-rounded individuals who show a robust intellectual appetite, devotion, or passion for the discipline, and the willingness to improve professionally.
